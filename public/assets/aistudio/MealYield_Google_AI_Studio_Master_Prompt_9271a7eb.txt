## Recipe 1 — Pakistani Aloo Keema

- **Cuisine:** Pakistani
- **Page title:** Pakistani Aloo Keema Recipe | Mince and Potato Curry
- **Slug:** `pakistani-aloo-keema`
- **Meta description:** Learn how to make comforting Pakistani aloo keema with minced meat, potatoes, tomatoes and aromatic spices. Includes a simple step-by-step method.
- **Source for culinary verification:** [Fatima Cooks — Pakistani Aloo Keema](https://fatimacooks.net/aloo-keema/)
- **Editorial angle:** A family-style curry that pairs well with warm roti. The potatoes absorb the savoury tomato-and-mince gravy.

### Ingredients — draft for 3–4 portions
- 500 g minced lamb or beef
- 2 medium potatoes, peeled and cut into bite-sized pieces
- 1 large onion, finely diced
- 4 small tomatoes, chopped
- 1 tbsp each minced garlic and ginger
- 3 tbsp neutral cooking oil
- 1 tsp ground cumin; 1½ tsp ground coriander
- ½ tsp turmeric; ½–1 tsp chilli powder; ½ tsp garam masala
- Salt to taste; fresh coriander and sliced green chillies to finish
- Approximately 250 ml water, added as needed

### Method — original editorial draft
1. Warm the oil in a heavy pot. Soften the onion over medium heat, then add garlic and ginger until fragrant.
2. Stir in tomatoes, ground spices and salt. Cook until the tomatoes break down and the mixture becomes thick and glossy.
3. Add the mince, break up lumps and cook until its raw colour has disappeared and it begins to brown.
4. Pour in enough water to prevent sticking, partially cover and simmer gently until the mince is nearly tender.
5. Add potatoes and continue simmering until fully tender and the gravy has reduced to the consistency you want. Finish with coriander and green chilli.

**Kitchen checks before publishing:** Test the liquid amount and simmer time with the selected mince and potato variety; ensure minced meat is fully cooked. **Image brief:** A close, well-lit hero photo of aloo keema in a deep green ceramic bowl with visible potato pieces, roti beside it. **Alt text:** “Pakistani aloo keema curry with minced meat and potatoes served with roti.”

## Recipe 2 — Indian Chana Masala (Weeknight Chickpea Curry)

- **Cuisine:** Indian
- **Page title:** Easy Indian Chana Masala | Chickpea Curry for Dinner
- **Slug:** `indian-chana-masala`
- **Meta description:** Make a weeknight chana masala with chickpeas, onions, tomatoes and warming spices. Serve the rich curry with rice or roti.
- **Source for culinary verification:** [Swasthi's Recipes — Chana Masala](https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/chana-masala/)
- **Editorial angle:** The version below is deliberately a quicker cooked-chickpea adaptation; don't describe it as the only traditional preparation.

### Ingredients — draft for 4 portions
- 2 x 400 g tins chickpeas, drained and rinsed (or an equivalent amount cooked from dried)
- 1 onion, finely chopped; 3 tomatoes, finely chopped
- 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste; 2 tbsp oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds; 1½ tsp ground coriander; ½ tsp turmeric
- ½–1 tsp chilli powder; 1 tsp garam masala
- 150–250 ml water; salt to taste
- Lemon, chopped coriander and sliced onion for serving

### Method — original editorial draft
1. Heat oil and briefly toast cumin seeds. Add onion and cook until it starts to turn golden.
2. Stir in ginger-garlic paste. Add tomatoes, salt and the ground spices except garam masala; cook to a thick, fragrant base.
3. Add chickpeas and water. Simmer until the sauce coats the chickpeas and the flavours combine, adding water for a looser gravy if desired.
4. Mash a small spoonful of chickpeas into the sauce for extra body. Finish with garam masala, lemon and coriander.

**Kitchen checks:** Taste-test spice amounts; note that canned and dried chickpeas need different handling. **Image brief:** Chana masala with coriander and lemon in an earthen bowl; basmati rice nearby. **Alt text:** “Indian chana masala chickpea curry garnished with fresh coriander.”

## Recipe 3 — Bangladeshi Bhuna Khichuri

- **Cuisine:** Bangladeshi
- **Page title:** Bangladeshi Bhuna Khichuri | Fragrant Rice and Lentils
- **Slug:** `bangladeshi-bhuna-khichuri`
- **Meta description:** Discover Bangladeshi bhuna khichuri, fragrant rice and lentils cooked with whole spices, caramelised onion and warming aromatics.
- **Source for culinary verification:** [Banglar Rannaghor — Dhaka Style Bhuna Dal Khichuri](https://www.banglarrannaghor.com/post/dhaka-style-bhuna-dal-khichuri-recipe). Also see [Bong Eats — Niramish Bhuna Khichuri](https://www.bongeats.com/recipe/niramish-bhuna-khichuri) for a distinct Bengali variation.
- **Editorial angle:** This dish has regional and household variations. Specify that the below is a **Dhaka-inspired draft**, not a definitive national recipe.

### Ingredients — small-batch development draft, 3–4 portions
- 1½ cups fragrant short-grain rice, rinsed and soaked
- ¼ cup yellow moong dal and ½ cup red lentils, rinsed
- 1 onion, thinly sliced; 1 tsp ginger paste; ½ tsp garlic paste
- 1½ tbsp mustard oil and 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 2 cardamom pods, 3 cloves, 1 small cinnamon stick, 1 bay leaf
- ½ tsp turmeric; ¼ tsp each ground coriander and chilli powder
- 3–3½ cups hot water as an **untested starting range**
- Salt, green chillies, fried onions and a little ghee to finish

### Method — original editorial draft
1. Drain the soaked rice and dal well. Cook sliced onion in warmed oils until light golden; add whole spices and stir briefly.
2. Add ginger, garlic, turmeric, coriander and chilli; stir with a splash of water to keep the spices from scorching.
3. Fold in rice and lentils, coating everything in the fragrant onion mixture.
4. Add hot water, salt and green chillies. Cover and gently cook, checking texture and liquid as it simmers.
5. Allow to rest off heat, then fluff gently. Scatter fried onions on top and add a little ghee if desired.

**Kitchen checks:** This scaled-down water ratio has **not been validated**; do a full test and adjust according to rice and dal used before publishing. **Image brief:** Golden bhuna khichuri in a shallow brass bowl, fried onions and green chillies. **Alt text:** “Bangladeshi bhuna khichuri made with fragrant rice and lentils.”

## Recipe 4 — Italian Saffron Risotto (Wine-Free Home Version)

- **Cuisine:** Italian
- **Page title:** Saffron Risotto Recipe | Creamy Italian Rice, No Wine
- **Slug:** `italian-saffron-risotto`
- **Meta description:** Make an Italian-inspired saffron risotto with creamy rice, warm stock, butter and cheese. A straightforward wine-free home variation.
- **Source for culinary verification:** [GialloZafferano — Saffron Risotto](https://www.giallozafferano.com/recipes/saffron-risotto.html)
- **Editorial angle:** Clearly label this a wine-free variation; don't conflate it with a strict traditional Milanese recipe.

### Ingredients — draft for 4 portions
- 320 g arborio or carnaroli rice
- 1 small onion or shallot, finely chopped
- 1 litre warm vegetable stock, plus a little extra if needed
- 1 small pinch saffron, steeped in a few spoons of warm stock
- 2 tbsp butter; 1 tbsp olive oil
- 50 g finely grated hard cheese suitable for the intended audience
- Salt and freshly ground pepper to taste

### Method — original editorial draft
1. Warm the stock in a separate pot and steep saffron in a little of it.
2. Gently cook the chopped onion in olive oil and some butter without burning it.
3. Stir in dry rice for a minute until warmed through. Add stock a ladle at a time, stirring often and letting each addition absorb before the next.
4. Add saffron stock midway through cooking. Continue until the rice is tender with a gentle bite and the mixture flows softly rather than forming a stiff mound.
5. Turn off the heat and beat in the remaining butter and grated cheese. Rest briefly and serve straight away.

**Kitchen checks:** Verify exact stock absorption, cook time and cheese dietary suitability. **Image brief:** Bright golden saffron risotto with delicate rice texture on a white ceramic plate. **Alt text:** “Creamy golden Italian saffron risotto served on a white plate.”

## Recipe 5 — British Cottage Pie

- **Cuisine:** British
- **Page title:** Classic British Cottage Pie | Beef and Mashed Potatoes
- **Slug:** `british-cottage-pie`
- **Meta description:** Try a comforting British cottage pie with savoury minced beef, vegetables and a golden mashed potato topping.
- **Source for culinary verification:** [BBC Good Food — Cottage Pie](https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/cottage-pie)
- **Editorial angle:** Cottage pie uses beef mince; shepherd's pie traditionally uses lamb. This is a no-wine home variation.

### Ingredients — development draft for 4 portions
- 500 g minced beef; 1 onion; 1 carrot; 1 celery stalk; 2 garlic cloves
- 1 tbsp tomato paste; 1 tbsp flour; 300 ml beef stock
- 1 tbsp olive oil; ½ tsp dried thyme; salt and pepper
- 750 g potatoes, peeled and cubed
- 2 tbsp butter; approximately 80 ml milk; optional grated cheddar

### Method — original editorial draft
1. Brown beef in a hot pan, then set aside. Soften chopped onion, carrot and celery; add garlic.
2. Stir in tomato paste and flour, return beef to the pan, add stock and thyme, and simmer until the gravy is thick rather than watery.
3. Boil potatoes until soft, drain thoroughly and mash with warm milk and butter. Season the mash.
4. Spread the beef mixture in a baking dish, spoon on the mash and create a rough surface with a fork. Add a little cheese if using.
5. Bake at approximately 200°C until the topping is browned and the filling is bubbling. Let stand briefly before serving.

**Kitchen checks:** Confirm the pan size, final baking time and safe cooked filling temperature in a test batch. **Image brief:** Baked cottage pie in a ceramic dish with golden ridged mash and a cut slice. **Alt text:** “Classic British cottage pie with golden mashed potato topping.”

## Recipe 6 — American Fluffy Buttermilk Pancakes

- **Cuisine:** American
- **Page title:** Fluffy Buttermilk Pancakes | American Breakfast Recipe
- **Slug:** `american-buttermilk-pancakes`
- **Meta description:** Make fluffy American buttermilk pancakes with a tender centre and golden edges. Includes tips for mixing and griddle heat.
- **Source for culinary verification:** [King Arthur Baking — Buttermilk Pancakes](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/buttermilk-pancakes-recipe)
- **Editorial angle:** A practical breakfast alternative to the site's existing burger recipe.

### Ingredients — draft for 4 portions
- 240 g plain flour; 3 tbsp sugar; 1 tsp baking powder; 1 tsp baking soda; ½ tsp salt
- 1 egg; 450 ml buttermilk; 30 g melted butter
- Optional vanilla; butter and fruit for serving

### Method — original editorial draft
1. Whisk flour, sugar, raising agents and salt in one bowl. Whisk egg, buttermilk and melted butter in another.
2. Stir wet into dry only until no large dry pockets remain. Small lumps are fine; overmixing makes pancakes tougher.
3. Heat a lightly greased griddle to medium heat. Spoon out small rounds and cook until bubbles appear and the lower surface is golden.
4. Flip once and cook through. Serve warm with fruit or your chosen topping.

**Kitchen checks:** Confirm actual yield for your ladle size and cooking temperature; keep cooked pancakes warm safely. **Image brief:** A stack of golden pancakes with blueberries and a small drizzle of syrup; natural light. **Alt text:** “Stack of fluffy American buttermilk pancakes with blueberries.”

## Original blog article draft A — How to Double a Recipe Without Doubling the Problems

**Slug:** `how-to-double-a-recipe`  
**Title:** How to Double a Recipe: Ingredients, Pan Size and Cooking Time  
**Meta description:** Scaling a recipe is more than multiplying ingredients. Learn how to adjust seasoning, pan size, liquid and cooking time for bigger batches.

Doubling a recipe is easy on paper: change a four-serving meal into eight servings, and multiply every ingredient by two. In the kitchen, a larger batch behaves differently. Crowded pans trap moisture, bigger pots take longer to heat, and a thick layer of food may cook unevenly.

Start with the ingredient list. Most core ingredients—such as rice, chickpeas and chopped vegetables—can be multiplied directly as a first calculation. Ingredients added for taste, especially salt and chilli, deserve a more cautious approach: begin with slightly less than the fully scaled amount, taste when safe, and adjust.

Next, check equipment capacity. A narrow pan that handles one onion and 250 grams of mince may steam instead of brown when filled with twice as much food. Brown meat in batches if necessary. For baked food, aim for a similar batter or filling depth rather than simply putting double the mixture into the original tin.

Don't assume the cooking time doubles. Some steps barely change; others need extra time because the food takes longer to heat through. Use recipe-specific signs of doneness and a suitable food thermometer where relevant. Finally, record what changed. Meal Yield's serving calculator helps with the arithmetic, but your pan, heat source and final batch size still matter.

**Related internal links to create:** recipe scaling calculator; Aloo Keema; British Cottage Pie. **Photo:** Two different-sized pots holding the same curry at different batch sizes.

## Original blog article draft B — Build Balanced Meals Without Giving Up Your Favourite Recipes

**Slug:** `balanced-meals-with-favourite-recipes`  
**Title:** How to Build a Balanced Meal Around Rice, Curry or Pasta  
**Meta description:** Learn practical ways to combine vegetables, protein and higher-fibre carbohydrates when serving everyday curries, rice dishes and pasta.

A balanced meal doesn't require abandoning your favourite foods. The useful question is what accompanies the main dish and how the overall pattern of eating adds up across the day or week.

If dinner is a hearty curry with rice, consider adding a generous vegetable side and paying attention to the quantity of rice and protein foods. For pasta, a salad or cooked vegetables can provide more variety. Beans and lentils are versatile ways to add plant protein and fibre to everyday meals.

The NHS Eatwell Guide suggests that fruit and vegetables and starchy carbohydrates should each make up just over a third of the overall diet. It also recommends choosing higher-fibre starchy foods where possible and including a variety of protein sources. That balance is a guide to overall eating habits, not a rule that every single plate must match exactly.

For cooking at home, three small habits are especially practical: plan vegetables at the same time as the main dish, serve sauces and toppings with awareness of portions, and use leftovers sensibly to avoid waste. These are general healthy-eating ideas, not personalised medical advice.

**Source:** [NHS Eatwell Guide](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/food-guidelines-and-food-labels/the-eatwell-guide/). **Related internal links to create:** chana masala; bhuna khichuri; healthy eating category. **Photo:** Family-style rice, curry, vegetables and lentils served together.

## SEO and publication checklist for the developer

- Each recipe needs a unique, crawlable URL (`/recipe/<slug>`), useful title, meta description and visible H1.
- Include genuinely useful copy: a description, an ingredients list with quantities, cooking steps, honest prep/cook times, serving yield, substitutions actually tested, storage safety, and useful troubleshooting.
- Use appropriate **Recipe** JSON-LD with image, title, ingredient, instructions, time and yield. Validate it in Google's Rich Results Test. Never invent star ratings or reviews.
- Google recommends recipe photos of the actual finished food, including suitable high-resolution versions with 1:1, 4:3 and 16:9 ratios where available.
- Photos must be original or clearly licensed for reuse. A source article's image is **not** automatically free to republish. AI-generated illustrative images may be used if accurately described, but should not be passed off as personally photographed or tested food.
- Provide accurate alt text, compress images (WebP/AVIF if appropriate), and avoid lazy-loading the main visible recipe image unnecessarily.
- Add internal links between cuisine category pages, relevant recipes, kitchen guides and scaling tools.
- Verify Googlebot can see the main recipe text after JavaScript rendering. React/Vite client rendering deserves a check in Search Console's URL Inspection Tool; prerendering or SSR may improve reliability if necessary.
- Publish a valid sitemap and check canonical tags, `robots.txt`, 200/404 response codes and mobile layout. Request indexing after publication; don't promise rankings.
- Don't set fabricated nutrition numbers or made-up chef biographies to fill existing template fields. Omit nutrition until calculated from validated quantities, and identify adaptations as such.
- Distinguish editorial review, recipe testing, sourcing, and publication dates.

**Technical source:** [Google Recipe structured-data documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/recipe), [Google JavaScript SEO guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics), [Google's helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

## Integrating with current React site

The earlier supplied `RecipeDetailView.tsx` component expects fields including `title`, `subtitle`, `description`, `cuisine`, `category`, `publishedDate`, `image`, `servings`, ingredient amounts and units, cooking steps, author name, tips, storage notes, nutrition and Pinterest metadata. The **source data file** (likely `src/data/recipes.ts`) and the exact `Recipe` TypeScript interface are necessary to safely convert these drafts into directly importable objects. The drafts above are therefore **not yet deployable JSON/TS** and are **not yet published**.

### Favicon installation in Vite

Copy `favicon.ico`, `favicon-32.png` and `apple-touch-icon.png` into the Vite application's `public/` directory. In `index.html` inside `<head>`, add or update:

```html
<link rel="icon" href="/favicon.ico" sizes="any" />
<link rel="icon" type="image/png" href="/favicon-32.png" sizes="32x32" />
<link rel="apple-touch-icon" href="/apple-touch-icon.png" />
```

Remove conflicting old favicon links, commit the changes, let deployment complete, and hard-refresh to check the browser tab icon.
